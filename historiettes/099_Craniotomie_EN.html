Craniotomy

2026.
I have to undergo a minor surgical procedure which, I am told, requires a craniotomy.
I think I know what a craniotomy is, and it seems entirely incompatible with the word minor.
Yet it is presented to me as a routine procedure.
They explain that the upper part of my skull will be cut away and removed for a few minutes so that some small spots on the surface of my brain can be cleaned.
Once the procedure is over, my skullcap will be put back in place and will naturally fuse back together, leaving no aftereffects or scars.
The thought of having my brain exposed like this worries me a little, but I am assured that everything will be fine, and for some reason my mistrust almost completely disappears.

Lying on a hospital bed, I see a nurse approaching, wearing a thick plexiglass visor and carrying an enormous circular saw. I am beginning to regret agreeing to this “minor” procedure.
Without a word, he starts the saw.
It makes a horrible noise, like pieces of metal clattering against one another.
The toothed disc begins to spin, creating a powerful current of air.
He brings it close to my skull while holding my head steady between his legs.
Then he works his way around my head in a series of acrobatic movements, taking improbable positions to handle the saw, which seems far too heavy for him.
I must stop watching him… but I don’t want to close my eyes either.
I stare at the wall and concentrate on my breathing.
Suddenly, I feel the saw level with my cheekbones. But it should be above my eyes. Has he lost his balance? Can he even see what he is doing through his fogged-up visor?
I have the impression that he is making another pass to cut higher up……
My whole body is rigid.
I want to run as far away as I can, but I barely dare to breathe, terrified that the slightest movement might make everything worse…

The nurse is gone. Did I lose consciousness?
A doctor is speaking calmly to me. He is holding my skullcap in his hands. He places it on a small metal tray covered with gauze.
My brain is exposed, yet I feel nothing. No coolness, no current of air.
I am calmer now, almost relaxed, as though after intense physical exertion.
After all, the worst is behind me.
A nurse calls the doctor away. He leaves, gesturing for me not to worry.
He’ll be right back…

Minutes pass, then hours…
I am now alone in my room, exhausted.
I feel my eyelids slowly closing.
I pinch myself regularly to stay awake.
I have only one fear: falling asleep, slumping down, and having my pillow stick to my brain.
Did I fall asleep?
I feel a tongue licking my face.
The drool running down my cheek and the sound of that tongue are unbearable.
I turn my head and see a misshapen creature covered in long brown hair.
It is licking my cheekbone now, its tongue slowly but surely moving upward.
It is trying to reach the top of my skull, my brain. I can feel it.
I throw myself at the creature, cover it with a blanket, grab its neck with both hands and squeeze as hard as I can.
Then I hear terrible guttural sounds, like desperate pleas.
I loosen my grip.
I drag the creature, still wrapped in the blanket, out of the room…
What kind of hospital is this?
What is happening to me?

The doctor finally returns with a whole group of nurses.
He tells me something or other. I’m not listening. I just want this to be over.
He takes a small brush, gives my brain two or three little strokes, then puts the missing part of my skull back in place…
