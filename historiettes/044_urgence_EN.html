Emergency

My son was always breaking his glasses at school.
A new optician’s had just opened in Paris. Their prices were unbeatable.
One afternoon, we decided to go there as a family.
The place was absolutely packed.
Hours of waiting.
Staff completely overwhelmed.
By the time our glasses were supposed to be ready, they still weren’t.
The hours went by and nobody called us.
Eventually, I joined the queue at one of the counters.
Just as someone was dealing with my request, a man interrupted us:
“Excuse me, I’m really in a hurry. My child is waiting for me in my car…”
I recognised him.
We had just spent more than two hours waiting at the same café terrace.
He had been our only neighbour; yet he hadn’t recognised us.
I looked at him and replied:
“And I really need to take a shit.”
Dead silence.
Everyone stopped talking. Nobody moved.
I handed over my prescription. They gave me my glasses immediately.
I politely said goodbye and walked out feeling strangely light.
With a smile on my face, I wondered how I could possibly have said something so crude, so loudly and in front of so many people, when I’m usually so reserved in public.
I also wonder why thinking about this story still makes me laugh,
when I can sometimes brood over some insignificant thing I said decades ago that everyone but me has probably long since forgotten.
