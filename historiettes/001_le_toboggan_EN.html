The Slide

Pyrenees. I’m nine years old, and as always, my cousins and I are spending the summer at our great-grandmother’s.

Just outside the village, on the banks of the Ariège, there was an almost always deserted municipal campsite with an abandoned slide.
The slide seemed enormous to us, and a little ghostly, lost among the trees.
It was rusty and rickety, had a few gaping holes in it, and was officially off-limits.
So we had even more reason to sneak over there and play.
One day, one of us had the idea of making a train down the slide.
I was at the front, holding back the others as they piled up behind me, impatient to go.
One of my espadrilles fell off.
I reached out to grab it.
At that very moment, a violent shove in the back threw me off balance.
I tumbled into the void,
saw my hands in front of my face.
Then blackness…
When I opened my eyes again, I was standing all alone,
near the campsite exit.
I had no idea what I was doing there.
I couldn’t feel anything, but I had the sense that something was wrong.
I looked at my left arm.
I couldn’t see my hand anymore.
My arm stopped abruptly at the elbow.
As I moved my head, I felt something tickling my ear.
Out of the corner of my eye, I saw fingers—my fingers…
My arm was bent backwards.
My disjointed hand was at the very top of my back, just behind my ear.
I knew they were going to have to cut off my arm.
I started walking like a puppet. Straight ahead.
I came across a fisherman sitting by the river.
Pointing to my elbow, I told him:
— I broke my arm. They’re going to cut it off…
He slowly turned his head towards me, looked at me calmly… then went back to fishing.

The next thing I remember is the village doctor’s office. It was late.
Nobody seemed to notice my arm.
Without asking me, they pulled down my trousers in front of everyone and shoved a painkilling suppository up my backside.

It was during the ambulance ride that the pain began.
The mountain road was winding, and every jolt was agony.

Then the hospital.
They made me wait for hours in the middle of an empty room tiled in grey.
I sat on a metal stool, wrapped in an old blanket, beneath a fluorescent light.
I was shivering. I was exhausted. I was in pain…
Finally, they laid me on an operating table, put a mask over my face and told me to count to a hundred.

I woke in the middle of the night with a weight on my stomach.
It was my left arm. It was there, heavy, bent the right way and encased in a thick plaster cast.
I couldn’t believe it was still there, whole.
Sitting beside the bed, my grandmother had fallen asleep.
Her head and arms rested heavily on my legs.

Through the window, I watched the sky.
It was completely black, with just a few tiny stars.
I felt old…
