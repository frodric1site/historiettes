Craniotomy

2026.
I have to undergo a minor surgical procedure which, I am told, requires a craniotomy.
I think I know what a craniotomy is, and it seems entirely incompatible with the word minor.
Yet it is presented to me as a routine operation.
I am told that the upper part of my skull will be cut away and removed for a few moments so that some small spots on the surface of my brain can be cleaned.
Once the operation is over, the top of my skull will be put back in place and will naturally fuse back together, leaving no aftereffects or scars.
The idea of having my brain exposed like that worries me a little, but I am assured that everything will be fine, and for some reason my mistrust almost completely disappears.

Lying on a hospital bed, I see a nurse approaching, wearing a thick Plexiglas visor and carrying an enormous circular saw. I am beginning to regret having agreed to this “minor” operation.
Without a word, he starts the saw.
It makes a horrible metallic clattering noise.
The toothed blade begins to whir, creating a powerful current of air.
He brings it close to my skull while holding my head steady between his legs.
Then he works his way around it, performing acrobatic movements and adopting improbable positions to handle a saw that seems far too heavy for him.
I must stop watching him… but I don’t want to close my eyes either.
I stare at the wall and concentrate on my breathing.
Suddenly, I feel the saw level with my cheekbones.
It should be above my eyes.
Has he lost his balance? Can he see what he is doing through his fogged-up visor?
I have the impression that he is making another pass to cut higher up…
I want to run away as fast as I can, but I barely dare to breathe, petrified by the thought that the slightest movement could make everything worse…
The nurse has disappeared. Did I lose consciousness?
A doctor is speaking to me calmly. He is holding the top of my skull in his hands. He places it on a small metal tray covered with gauze.
My brain is exposed, yet I feel nothing, neither coolness nor any current of air.
I am calm, completely relaxed, as though after intense physical exertion.
After all, the worst is behind me.
A nurse calls the doctor away. He walks off, gesturing to me not to worry.
He’ll be right back…

The minutes pass, then the hours…
I am now alone in my room.
My eyelids slowly begin to close.
I pinch myself regularly to stay awake…
Because every time I doze off, the same vision wakes me in terror: I fall asleep, slump down, and my pillowcase sticks to my brain.

Did I fall asleep?
I feel a tongue licking my face.
My cheek is covered in drool, and the sound of that tongue is unbearable.
I turn my head and see a misshapen creature with long brown hair.
It is licking my cheekbone now, its tongue moving slowly but surely upward.
It is trying to reach the top of my skull, my brain. I can feel it.
I snap out of my stupor, throw myself on it, cover it with a blanket, grab its neck with both hands and squeeze as hard as I can.
Then I hear heartrending guttural sounds, like desperate pleas.
I loosen my grip.
I pull the creature, still wrapped in the blanket, and drag it out of the room…
What kind of hospital is this?
What is happening to me?

The doctor finally returns with a whole procession of nurses.
He tells me something or other. I am not listening; I just want it to be over.
He takes a small brush, gives my brain two or three little strokes, and puts the missing part of my skull back in place…

Long after writing Craniotomy, I came across a quote falsely attributed to Nietzsche:
“Strike the monster while it is still a monster. Once you understand its pain, your hand will begin to weaken.”
It immediately reminded me of the creature in my story.
More curiously still, Nietzsche actually wrote a passage in Thus Spoke Zarathustra in which Zarathustra hears a “gloomy monster” groaning, imagines that it is suffering from bad memories or bad dreams, and regrets that his hand is not strong enough to free it from its nightmares. Then he makes fun of himself: he would want to caress any monster as soon as he sensed something living in it.
