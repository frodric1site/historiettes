The scorpion and the tortoise

A scorpion came to the bank of a river.
Unable to cross, he found himself in quite a predicament.
A tortoise, seeing him pacing back and forth and staring at the opposite bank, approached him.
She invited him to climb onto her back, entered the water, and began swimming toward the other side.
During the crossing, she felt little taps on her back.
She asked him what they were.
The scorpion replied:
— It's my stinger. It can't pierce your shell, but I can't help trying…
The tortoise plunged beneath the water.
The scorpion was swept away by the current and disappeared without a trace…

Based on Jami's Baharistan (1487), Eighth Garden, Fable II
Freely adapted by Frodric

The scorpion and the frog

A scorpion came to the bank of a river.
Unable to cross, he found himself in quite a predicament.
He spotted a frog swimming nearby and asked her to carry him on her back.
— No, said the frog. You might sting me.
— Why would I do that? It would be suicidal…
The frog had to admit he was right.
She let the scorpion climb onto her back and began to swim.
The scorpion remained still for a while.
Then, halfway across, he plunged his stinger into the frog's back.
— What have you done? Now we're both going to die.
As they disappeared beneath the water, the scorpion replied:
— I know…

Based on an Eastern tale recorded by Lev Nitoburg in The German Quarter (1933)
Freely adapted by Frodric
English translation by ChatGPT-5.6
