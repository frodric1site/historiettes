Everyone's a doctor

One morning, at the Duke’s table, they were discussing which profession had the largest number of representatives in Ferrara.
The Duke asked Gonnella what he thought.
— Doctors are the most numerous!
The Duke replied:
— You obviously don’t know the city very well. There are only two or three doctors in Ferrara.
— It seems to me that you’re the one who doesn’t know your own city very well.
To prove it, Gonnella proposed a bet, which the Duke accepted without hesitation…
Early the next morning, Gonnella went to stand in front of the cathedral, with a bandage wrapped around his head that made him look like a giant Easter egg.
Everyone who saw him asked what was wrong.
Holding his cheek, he told them he had a terrible toothache.
Each of them immediately recommended a remedy known only to them…
After thanking them warmly, Gonnella carefully wrote down their names and their prescriptions.
He then made his way through the rest of the city.
By the end of the morning, he already had a list of more than three hundred people.
At lunchtime, he returned to the palace, still bandaged and pretending to be in agony.
As soon as the Duke heard about it, he too recommended a remedy.
Gonnella put the Duke’s name at the very top of his list.
The next day, he returned to see the Duke without his bandage.
He reminded him of their bet, took out his list and handed it to him.
The Duke first read his own name, followed by all the others, together with every remedy imaginable.
He burst out laughing, admitted that he had lost, and had Gonnella paid.

Italian jest recounted by Lodovico Domenichi in the 16th century

About this tale
This jest was recorded in the 16th century by Lodovico Domenichi, who attributed it to Gonnella, the famous Italian jester around whom many stories circulated.
It appears in various forms and with different characters in collections of jests from the period.
