The four mad brahmins : the first brahmin

Here is what the first Brahmin had to say:
As you can see, I am dressed in rags.
The story I am about to tell you will explain why.
A wealthy cloth merchant, who was very generous to Brahmins, had once given me two magnificent pieces of cloth. I proudly showed them to all my fellow Brahmins, who congratulated me and assured me that this gift was the reward for good deeds performed in a previous life.
Before wearing them, I washed them, as was customary, to purify them of any impurities left by the hands of the weaver and the merchant. Then I hung them from the branches of a tree to dry.
A dog appeared out of nowhere and walked beneath them. As I had been unable to see whether it had touched my cloth, I questioned my children, who were playing nearby.
Alas, they had seen nothing.
How could I find out whether the dog had defiled my cloth?
After much thought, I came up with an idea: I would get down on all fours, so that I was as tall as the dog, and pass beneath my cloth in that position.
Which I did.
“Did my back touch the cloth?” I asked my children, who were watching me wide-eyed.
“No,” they all answered in chorus.
Hearing this, I jumped for joy.
But a doubt soon assailed me: the dog had a tail.
If held upright, it might very well have touched my cloth and defiled it.
What was I to do? I tied a palm leaf to my backside, as long as the dog's tail, made sure it was pointing upwards, and passed beneath my cloth once more.
“The leaf touched! The leaf touched!” cried my children, delighted by this new game.
I tore off the leaf, threw it to the ground, and stamped on it furiously.
There could no longer be any doubt that my cloth was defiled.
Overcome with despair, I took down my cloth, tore it into tiny pieces, and cursed the dog, its master, and the entire universe a thousand times over.
News of my misadventure spread throughout the region, and soon everyone was calling me a fool.
“Couldn't you have washed it a second time to remove the impurity?” people would ask.
“You should have given it to the poor!” another would add.
“Who will ever give you clothes now?” a third would ask.
And that is why I have been dressed in rags ever since.
When he had finished his story, one of the listeners said to him:
“So, judging by your story, you seem to have become quite an expert at walking on all fours?”
“Oh, yes!” he replied. “Just watch…
” And the Brahmin began walking on all fours, his head held high and perfectly straight.
The entire assembly burst out laughing.
“That will do,” said the judge. “What you have just told us, and what we have just seen, both speak in your favour. But let us hear what your opponents have to say.” He motioned for the next Brahmin to speak…

Conte indien adapté par Frodric, d’après une traduction de l’abbé Dubois
